Re: Request for public guidance on whether Kansas cast vote records are open to the public under the Kansas Open Records Act, and for help preserving 2026 election records under the Guarantee Clause framing of Executive Order 14399

Dear Attorney General Kobach:

## 1. Who I am and why I am writing

"The United States shall guarantee to every State in this Union a Republican Form of Government." U.S. Const. art. IV, § 4. A republican form of government rests on elections the public can check for itself. I write to respectfully ask your office to help Kansas county election officers and the public do exactly that with the records Kansas law already creates.

I am Morissa Benson, and I write regarding public access to Kansas election records. I have studied the official 2022 and the unofficial 2026 Kansas governor results, county by county, using only data from the Kansas Secretary of State. I am not alleging that any Kansas county election officer, county clerk, election commissioner, vendor, or employee did anything improper, and I am not alleging that any Kansas result is wrong. I am asking for two things: (1) a clear public statement of how the Kansas Open Records Act applies to cast vote records, and (2) your office's help in making sure the records needed to answer questions about the November 3, 2026 general election are preserved and made available as the law provides.

## 2. The federal framing: Executive Order 14399

On March 31, 2026, the President signed Executive Order 14399, "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections," 91 Fed. Reg. 17125 (Apr. 3, 2026). Its authority clause rests in part on "the Federal Government's constitutional obligation to guarantee a republican form of Government to every State in the Union, U.S. Const. Art. IV, Sec. 4." Section 1 adds that "[t]he Federal Government has an unavoidable duty under Article II of the Constitution of the United States to enforce Federal law, which includes preventing violations of Federal criminal law and maintaining public confidence in election outcomes." That duty traces to the President's charge to "take Care that the Laws be faithfully executed." U.S. Const. art. II, § 3.

Section 5 of the Order states that "States and localities should preserve, for a 5-year period, all records and materials—excluding ballots cast—evidencing voter participation in any Federal election (e.g., ballot envelopes, regardless of carrier)."

I recognize the limits of that framing. The Order directs federal officers, not your office; Section 5's preservation language is phrased as "should"; Section 7(c) says the Order creates no enforceable right; and parts of the Order have been in litigation. I cite it not as a command to Kansas but because it states, at the federal level, the same public interest the Kansas Legislature declared when it adopted the Open Records Act: "It is declared to be the public policy of the state that public records shall be open for inspection by any person unless otherwise provided by this act, and this act shall be liberally construed and applied to promote such policy." K.S.A. 45-216(a).

## 3. What Kansas law already provides

Electronic records are public records. The Open Records Act defines a "public record" as "any recorded information, regardless of form, characteristics or location, that is made, maintained or kept by or is in the possession of" any public agency. K.S.A. 45-217(l)(1). A request "shall be acted upon as soon as possible, but not later than the end of the third business day following the date that the request is received," and if access is denied, the custodian must, upon request, provide a written statement that "shall cite the specific provision of law under which access is denied." K.S.A. 45-218(d). Where a record holds both open and closed material, the agency "shall separate or delete such material and make available to the requester that material in the public record that is subject to disclosure." K.S.A. 45-221(d).

No exception names cast vote records. I could find no exception in K.S.A. 45-221 that mentions ballots, ballot images, or cast vote records. The question turns instead on K.S.A. 45-221(a)(1), which covers records whose disclosure is "specifically prohibited or restricted by federal law, state statute," and on the Election Code. K.S.A. 25-2422(a)(1) makes it a crime for a person charged with an election duty to intentionally disclose or expose "the contents of any ballot, whether cast in a regular or provisional manner, or the name of any voter who cast such ballot," except as ordered by a court in an election contest. Separately, K.S.A. 25-1122(i) provides that "[a]ll records made by the county election officer shall be subject to public inspection," with listed exceptions for voter identification information and ballot numbers.

The Legislative Division of Post Audit found the law unsettled. In its February 2023 report, *Reviewing Kansas's Procedures for Election Security, Part 1*, it reported that Kansas county election officials "held ballot images as closed records," and that "State law doesn't address cast vote records, so it isn't clear they should be treated like paper ballots."

Preservation rules. K.S.A. 25-2708(b) requires the county election officer to "preserve all county, city, school district and township ballots for six months and all state and national ballots for 22 months." Your office's Opinion No. 2025-25 (Dec. 16, 2025) concluded that the duty to destroy ballots after that period "is directory and not mandatory, and such duty must give way to a search warrant or subpoena compelling the production or preservation of such ballots." Federal law separately requires that records of elections for federal office be kept for 22 months. 52 U.S.C. § 20701.

Tabulation, testing, and audit records. Each county must test its optical scanning equipment before the election, and "[t]he test shall be repeated within five business days after the completion of the canvass. The programs and ballots shall be sealed, retained and disposed of in the same manner as paper ballots." K.S.A. 25-4610(b). The printed return of the optical scanning equipment, with write-in, advance, and manually counted votes added, "shall constitute the official return of each precinct or voting area. Upon completion of the count the returns shall be open to the public." K.S.A. 25-4611(e). Before the county canvass, the county election officer must conduct a manual audit "in 1% of all precincts, with a minimum of one precinct located within the county," in a public setting, and the results are reported to the county election office and the Secretary of State; if a discrepancy cannot be resolved, "the county election officer or the secretary of state may require audits of additional precincts." K.S.A. 25-3009(a), (c), (d). No voting system may be used until the Secretary of State certifies it, "including operating systems, firmware and software." K.S.A. 25-4404.

Where Kansas has no exact parallel. Two points deserve candor. First, I did not find a Kansas statute that names cast vote records or ballot images at all, either to open them or to close them. Second, I did not find a Kansas statute that, in so many words, requires a county to record or retain hash-validation results for its voting system or to keep a copy of the system's software for a set period. Whether a cast vote record that contains no voter's name is "the contents of any ballot" under K.S.A. 25-2422, and how K.S.A. 45-221(d) applies to it, is where guidance from your office would help most.

## 4. Why this matters for Kansas now

My county-level comparison of the 2022 and 2026 governor races covers all 105 Kansas counties and was checked cell by cell against the Secretary of State's own files. Kansas has party registration, so I compare each county's Democratic share of governor votes with its Democratic share of registered voters. The 2026 figures are unofficial until the state canvass is certified; certification is scheduled for December 1, 2026. What I have is an analytical model and a hypothesis, not proof. The only way to test a hypothesis like mine fairly is with the underlying records: cast vote records, ballot images where kept, and the testing and audit records described above. If those records show nothing unusual, that is a result worth having too.

## 5. Specific requests

I respectfully ask your office to:

1. State publicly, in whatever form your office considers appropriate, whether cast vote records from the November 3, 2026 general election are public records subject to inspection and copying under the Kansas Open Records Act once the county canvass is complete, and whether K.S.A. 25-2422 or any other statute restricts disclosure of a cast vote record that contains no voter's name or other identifying information. I understand that formal opinions under K.S.A. 75-704 are given on questions submitted by the officials that statute names, so I ask for public guidance rather than a formal opinion.
2. State your office's view on whether a custodian whose tabulation system can export cast vote records must provide an existing export in response to a request, and how K.S.A. 45-221(d) applies if part of a record is closed.
3. Remind county election officers, in coordination with the Secretary of State, that ballots, cast vote records, ballot images, the post-canvass test programs and ballots under K.S.A. 25-4610(b), and the audit records under K.S.A. 25-3009 for the 2026 general election should be kept for at least the full 22-month period, and consider encouraging the five-year retention of participation records described in Section 5 of Executive Order 14399.
4. Use the investigative authority of K.S.A. 45-228, if your office finds it warranted, to review whether counties are responding to requests for 2026 election records as the Open Records Act requires, and share the results with the public.

I would be grateful for a written response within 30 days of the date of this letter. I am glad to provide my county-level data and methods to your staff.

## 6. Closing

Kansans, and the officials who run their elections, deserve records that let anyone check the count. Kansas law already declares that public records should be open. A clear statement from your office would help county clerks and election commissioners apply the law the same way in all 105 counties, and it would help the public trust the result, whatever it shows.

Thank you for your attention to this request.

Respectfully,

Morissa Benson
